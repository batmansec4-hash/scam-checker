Fake BISP messages: how to tell what is real

Messages about BISP payments, new relief amounts or lucky draws spread fast, especially around Eid. Many are fake and exist to collect your CNIC, wallet details or a fee.

Warning signs

  • The sender is a personal mobile number, not 8171.
  • A lucky draw, prize or bonus. BISP has said it does not send prize messages.
  • A fee, or a link to register for assistance.
  • A request for your CNIC, wallet number or OTP.

What to do

  • A BISP spokesperson has said genuine BISP messages come only from 8171. Treat any other sender as fake.
  • Do not click links or call numbers in the message.
  • Check your eligibility only through official BISP channels that you reach yourself.
  • Report fake messages through the PTA complaint system.

Based on a BISP spokesperson's statement reported by Radio Pakistan and on NCCIA financial fraud guidance. Check official pages for current details.
